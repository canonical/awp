# Workshop 2 — Risk Origins

Answers: **who or what** can harm the missions and business assets from W1, **and for what purpose?** Identifies, characterises, and selects the most relevant **risk origin / target objective (RO/TO) pairs**, which seed the scenarios in W3 and W4.

## Participants
Top management (at least for the final selection step); business teams; CISO; optionally a digital-threat-analysis specialist.

## Outputs
- List of **priority RO/TO pairs** selected for the rest of the study.
- List of **secondary RO/TO pairs** to revisit later / keep under watch.
- A **mapping of risk origins**.

## Steps
a. Identify risk origins and target objectives.
b. Assess the RO/TO pairs.
c. Select the priority pairs.

Variable length: ~2 hours to one working day.

Needs from W1: the missions and business assets. Good characterisation needs current threat intelligence — ideally sector-specific (attacker groups, assumed resources and motivation, attack methods, most-exposed activities). Daily cyber-threat bulletins and security news help contextualise.

---

## a. Identify risk origins (RO) and target objectives (TO)
Ask:
- Which risk origins can harm the organisation's missions or high-level interests (sector, state, …)?
- For each RO, what are the **target objectives** in terms of the effect sought?

Review categories of RO and TO; for each RO category determine the attacker profile and the kinds of objectives pursued. One RO can yield **several** RO/TO pairs with different TO natures.

Keys to success:
- Seek **varied** RO/TO categories — a differentiated panel of attacker profiles and objectives.
- Leave **no blind spots** — cover the business assets as widely as possible.
- A TO can lie **beyond** the studied object; the object may then be an intermediary or suffer collateral impact.

**Risk origin** examples: state services, hacktivists, competitors, cyber-terrorists, vengeful employees. **Target objective** examples: information theft (lucrative/industrial spying), spreading an ideological message, revenge, provoking a health crisis.

Record as a table: *Risk origins | Target objectives*.

## b. Assess the RO/TO pairs
Once the team stops producing new pairs, rate each pair's relevance. Beyond participants' feedback, use characterisation criteria/metrics for objectivity:
- **Motivation** of the RO to reach the target,
- **Resources** (financial, skills, attack infrastructure),
- **Activity** (is it active in the object's perimeter, the ecosystem, the industry, a similar industry?).

A common shorthand is a "+ / ++ / +++" scoring per criterion, producing an overall **pertinence** (e.g. Low / Moderate / High).

## c. Select the priority RO/TO pairs
Finalise by selecting pairs for the rest of the study. Choice criteria: the assessed relevance, plus favouring pairs that are **sufficiently distinct** and likely to affect **different** business and supporting assets. Volume: **3–6 RO/TO pairs** is generally a sufficient base for W3. Set the rest aside as secondary (optionally under watch).

### Worked-example shape (vaccine biotech)
Pairs considered: Hacktivist → sabotage the vaccination campaign (Moderate); Competitor → steal R&D information for advantage (High); Hacktivist → disclose animal-test info (Low); Cyber-terrorist → alter vaccine composition for bioterrorism (Low). The team kept the High and Moderate pairs and set aside the two Low ones (though management later asked to keep the bioterrorist threat under surveillance).
